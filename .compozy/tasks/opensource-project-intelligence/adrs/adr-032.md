# ADR-032: Version Metric Cohorts, Health Rubrics, Trends, and Forecasts

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-21

## Context

Comparability requires more than naming a window. Each metric needs an event cohort, boundary rule,
eligibility rule, missing-data treatment, and calculation version. Health, trends, and forecasts
must remain deterministic and cannot change because an LLM, cohort membership, or missing value
changed.

## Decision

All windows are half-open UTC intervals `[from, to)` with an immutable cutoff. Daily snapshots use
UTC calendar days. Releases count published, non-draft, non-prerelease releases. Active contributors
and top-three concentration use human authors of eligible non-merge commits reachable from included
default branches; unresolved identities remain distinct and publish resolution coverage.

Issue first-response time covers issues opened in the window and ends at the first public, non-bot
response by a recognized member or collaborator other than the opener. Unanswered issues are
censored and reported as coverage, never zero. PR merge time covers PRs merged in the window and
measures `ready_for_review` to `merged_at`, falling back to `created_at` with explicit coverage.
Backlog variation is open-at-end minus open-at-start, reconstructed across reopen transitions.

The initial overall health definition gives equal weight to Activity, Community, Maintenance,
Concentration, Stability, Security, and Adoption. Each dimension uses absolute, published, versioned
rubrics. The overall result exists only when every mandatory dimension meets its coverage threshold;
weights are never redistributed for missing values.

Observed trends use versioned Theil-Sen slope and Mann-Kendall consistency rules with per-metric
sample and seasonality requirements. Forecasts use deterministic exponential-smoothing and seasonal
baselines, rolling backtests, error thresholds, and prediction intervals. LLMs may explain but never
create, suppress, or change these signals.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Cohort-relative health

- **Description**: Score each Project by percentile among currently registered Projects.
- **Pros**: Adapts thresholds automatically.
- **Cons**: A portfolio membership change alters unrelated historical scores.
- **Why rejected**: Health must remain reproducible and comparable over time.

### Alternative 2: LLM-derived trend and forecast

- **Description**: Ask a model to infer momentum and future behavior from data.
- **Pros**: Flexible narrative reasoning.
- **Cons**: Non-deterministic, hard to backtest, and violates product rules.
- **Why rejected**: Numerical signals must have reproducible definitions.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Every result is auditable from events, identities, windows, and definition versions.
- Comparisons cannot silently mix incompatible populations or missing values.

### Negative

- Metric definitions require substantial fixtures and migration discipline.
- Equal initial weights are simple rather than context-specific.

### Risks

- Provider event gaps can bias cohorts; every result publishes source and eligibility coverage.
- Statistical signals can look authoritative on weak history; minimum-history and backtest gates
  suppress publication rather than manufacture confidence.

## Implementation Notes

Store definition versions and result factors in normalized side tables. Store an immutable JSON
render snapshot only for historical explanation, not as the queryable calculation source.
