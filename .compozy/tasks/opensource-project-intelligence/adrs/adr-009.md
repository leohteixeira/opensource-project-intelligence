# ADR-009: Separate Observed Trends From Predictive Early Warnings

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-20

## Context

The product must report historical changes and also provide prediction or early-warning signals.
Those are different claims: an observed trend summarizes measured history, while an early warning
estimates a future risk. Presenting both under one label would make forecasts appear factual and
would obscure the uncertainty and evaluation requirements of predictive behavior.

## Decision

The product will expose two distinct signal classes:

- Observed trends use deterministic rules or reproducible statistical calculations over explicit
  observation and baseline windows.
- Predictive early warnings use versioned statistical models and always disclose their forecast
  horizon, confidence, calibration or known error, input coverage, model version, and supporting
  evidence.

The interface and API will never use the same label or status for an observed trend and a forecast.
Language models may explain a calculated signal but may not create, suppress, or change it.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Language-Model Detection

- **Description**: Ask an LLM to infer both trends and emerging risks from all available evidence.
- **Pros**: Flexible interpretation of quantitative and unstructured data.
- **Cons**: Non-reproducible detection, weak calibration, and risk of unsupported forecasts.
- **Why rejected**: The product requires auditable signals and graceful operation without an LLM.

### Alternative 2: Observed Trends Only

- **Description**: Remove predictive and early-warning behavior.
- **Pros**: Avoids forecast uncertainty and model evaluation requirements.
- **Cons**: Omits an explicitly selected part of the complete product vision.
- **Why rejected**: The user retained prediction and early-warning capabilities.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Users can distinguish measured change from estimated future risk.
- Predictive performance can be evaluated and calibrated independently of trend correctness.
- LLM outages do not prevent signal calculation.

### Negative

- The product must support separate terminology, evaluation datasets, and lifecycle rules.
- Predictive warnings require ongoing performance measurement as outcomes become known.

### Risks

- Confidence may be mistaken for probability unless labels and explanations define it precisely.
- Sparse histories may produce unstable forecasts; minimum evidence requirements must yield an
  explicit insufficient-data state.

## Implementation Notes

Stage 2 will define supported detectors, model evaluation, version retention, recalculation,
minimum-history requirements, and the public representation of both signal classes.
