# ADR-012: Limit HITL Assistant Execution to Non-Destructive Analyst Actions

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-20

## Context

The natural-language experience is intended to do more than answer read-only analytical questions.
Human-in-the-loop approval can make operational assistance safe enough for selected mutations, but
confirmation alone does not justify exposing credentials, governance, or destructive operations to
an interpretation generated from natural language.

## Decision

The assistant may propose and, after explicit per-action human approval, execute only
non-destructive operations available to the `Analyst` role. Supported actions include registering or
editing Projects and Repositories, requesting synchronization or reanalysis, managing alert rules,
and adding radar annotations.

Before approval, the product must show the exact operation, affected resources, supplied values,
expected effect, and any relevant quota or staleness consequence. Approval applies only to that
displayed operation and cannot authorize later or broader actions. Execution uses the requesting
user's identity and permissions, and both approval and result enter the audit log.

The assistant may not manage users, roles, source or model credentials, policy definitions or
versions, archives, permanent deletion, or any operation reserved to `Admin`.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Execute Every Role-Authorized Action With Confirmation

- **Description**: Permit administration and destructive actions when the requesting user has the
  role and confirms the proposal.
- **Pros**: Maximum conversational coverage.
- **Cons**: An ambiguous or manipulated prompt could reach credentials, governance, or irreversible
  operations.
- **Why rejected**: HITL reduces but does not eliminate the risk of high-impact natural-language
  actions.

### Alternative 2: Draft Actions Without Executing Them

- **Description**: Let the assistant prepare values while users complete every action in the normal
  interface.
- **Pros**: Strong separation between language interpretation and mutation.
- **Cons**: Adds friction to frequent, reversible Analyst workflows.
- **Why rejected**: Bounded execution with exact per-action approval provides useful automation with
  a controlled risk surface.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Users can complete common workflows conversationally without granting the assistant administrative
  authority.
- Every mutation remains explicit, attributed, reviewable, and auditable.
- A confirmation cannot be reused as standing approval.

### Negative

- Some journeys prepared in conversation must still be completed through conventional Admin UI.
- The product must present a precise action preview and detect any change between preview and
  execution.

### Risks

- A stale preview could apply to changed state; Stage 2 must define optimistic concurrency or an
  equivalent precondition check.
- Multiple actions hidden inside one request could weaken consent; approval must remain atomic and
  action-specific.

## Implementation Notes

Stage 2 will freeze the action catalog, typed proposal contract, approval lifecycle, expiration,
authorization checks, audit representation, and failure behavior. Free-form tool access is not part
of the product.
