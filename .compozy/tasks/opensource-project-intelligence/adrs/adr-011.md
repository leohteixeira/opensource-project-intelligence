# ADR-011: Derive the Technology Radar From Versioned Adoption Policies

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-20

## Context

The complete product includes both deterministic adoption recommendations and a workspace technology
radar. A separately scored or AI-generated radar would create another source of truth for the same
decision. A completely manual radar would preserve organizational judgment but could drift away from
the product's current evidence and approved policies.

## Decision

The workspace technology radar will derive each selected Project's suggested ring from an explicit,
versioned adoption policy evaluation. The mapping from the four recommendation outcomes to radar
rings will be visible and configurable by an Admin as part of policy governance.

Analysts choose which projects appear, add context, assign an owner and review date, and may record
a manual ring override with a required justification. An override does not change or replace the
underlying recommendation; both remain visible with their authorship and timestamps.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Fully Manual Radar

- **Description**: Analysts place and move projects without a required relationship to product
  metrics or recommendations.
- **Pros**: Maximum room for organizational context that the product does not collect.
- **Cons**: Radar positions can become stale, inconsistent, and difficult to audit.
- **Why rejected**: A policy-derived suggestion preserves evidence while explicit overrides retain
  human judgment.

### Alternative 2: AI-Generated Radar

- **Description**: Ask a language model to select radar rings from available intelligence.
- **Pros**: Can incorporate qualitative evidence flexibly.
- **Cons**: Creates non-deterministic decisions and duplicates the policy engine.
- **Why rejected**: Adoption decisions must remain deterministic and auditable.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Radar positions remain connected to current, reproducible evidence.
- Human exceptions are possible without hiding disagreement with the configured policy.
- Review dates and ownership make the radar an actionable governance surface.

### Negative

- Policy changes may create new suggested rings while manual overrides remain in place.
- The product must communicate recommendation, suggested ring, and overridden ring distinctly.

### Risks

- Teams may let overrides persist after their rationale expires; review dates and stale indicators
  must make unattended exceptions visible.
- A simplistic ring mapping could imply more precision than the policy outcome; all mappings must be
  explicit and versioned.

## Implementation Notes

Stage 2 will define radar ring configuration, recalculation after policy changes, override review,
and the exact UI/API representation.
