# ADR-007: Use Four Explicit Adoption Recommendation Outcomes

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-20

## Context

ADR-003 establishes deterministic, policy-driven adoption guidance but leaves the result contract
open. A binary verdict cannot distinguish a contextual concern from a conclusive rejection, while a
numeric score alone does not tell a technical decision-maker what the configured policy concluded.
Missing evidence must also remain different from negative evidence.

## Decision

Every completed adoption policy evaluation will produce exactly one of four outcomes:

- `recommended`: the evidence satisfies the policy's adoption conditions.
- `conditional`: adoption may be appropriate only under the displayed constraints or mitigations.
- `not_recommended`: the evidence violates one or more policy rejection conditions.
- `insufficient_data`: required evidence is unavailable or too stale to reach another result.

Every outcome must display the policy identity and version, observation window, evaluated inputs,
weights and thresholds where applicable, decisive factors, evidence references, and missing or stale
data. The product will include a transparent default policy. Admins may clone and version policies;
Analysts may select an available version but cannot edit it.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Numeric Fit Score Only

- **Description**: Return a continuous suitability score without a categorical conclusion.
- **Pros**: Supports sorting and preserves small differences.
- **Cons**: Leaves interpretation unclear and may imply precision unsupported by the evidence.
- **Why rejected**: Users need an explicit but auditable policy conclusion.

### Alternative 2: Pass or Fail

- **Description**: Return one binary adoption verdict.
- **Pros**: Simplest possible decision surface.
- **Cons**: Conflates conditional risks and missing evidence with rejection.
- **Why rejected**: It cannot express the required decision states safely.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Policy results are clear, reproducible, and comparable without hiding uncertainty.
- `insufficient_data` prevents absent evidence from being interpreted as poor performance.
- `conditional` lets policies communicate explicit mitigations instead of overstating rejection.

### Negative

- Policies must define precedence when different rules imply different outcomes.
- Existing results must retain their policy version when a newer version is published.

### Risks

- Users may read a recommendation as universal advice; every result must retain workspace policy
  context and limitations.
- Weakly specified conditions could make outcomes unstable; policy validation and deterministic test
  fixtures are required in Stage 2.

## Implementation Notes

Stage 2 will define policy validation, evaluation precedence, version activation, recalculation, and
the exact HTTP and UI representation of this four-state contract.
