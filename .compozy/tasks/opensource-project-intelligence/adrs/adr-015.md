# ADR-015: Consume a Workspace-Shared Keycloak and Keep Product Authorization Local

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-20

## Context

ADR-001 originally assigned authentication and authorization to this product without selecting an
identity mechanism. The workspace contains three independent products that will evaluate one shared
Keycloak deployment. Owning separate credentials and account lifecycles in each product would
duplicate identity concerns, while sharing product roles or data boundaries through the identity
system would couple otherwise independent repositories.

## Decision

Open Source Project Intelligence will consume authentication from a Keycloak deployment shared by
the three workspace products. Keycloak deployment, lifecycle, realm-level decisions, mail delivery,
and any dedicated shared-infrastructure repository are global workspace concerns and are not owned
or implemented by this repository.

This product will own its local membership and authorization. A successfully authenticated Keycloak
identity has no workspace access until an Open Source Project Intelligence Admin approves it. After
approval, the product assigns exactly one local `Admin`, `Analyst`, or `Viewer` role as defined by
ADR-004. Identity claims establish who the user is; they do not grant cross-product roles or access
to this product's data by themselves.

This ADR supersedes only the authentication-ownership portion of ADR-001. ADR-001's requirement for
explicit application authorization, protected surfaces, and accountable user actions remains in
force.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Product-Owned Accounts and Sessions

- **Description**: Implement registration, password storage, recovery, and sessions independently in
  this repository.
- **Pros**: Complete autonomy and no shared identity dependency.
- **Cons**: Duplicates identity infrastructure across three products and conflicts with the selected
  shared Keycloak direction.
- **Why rejected**: Identity is intentionally a global workspace capability.

### Alternative 2: Shared Keycloak Roles Grant Product Access Directly

- **Description**: Encode all product membership and roles centrally in Keycloak.
- **Pros**: One administrative surface for access across products.
- **Cons**: Couples product-specific permissions and approval workflows to global identity
  configuration.
- **Why rejected**: Each independent product must own its authorization and audit boundary.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Users can share one identity across the workspace products.
- This repository avoids owning passwords, registration verification, and global identity recovery.
- Product roles and approvals can evolve without granting equivalent access elsewhere.

### Negative

- Interactive access depends on the external shared Keycloak being available and correctly
  configured.
- Local membership must remain synchronized with stable external subject identity.
- End-to-end development needs an identity fixture or shared development deployment.

### Risks

- Global realm or claim changes could break all three products; the shared identity contract must be
  versioned and coordinated outside this repository.
- Conflating authentication with local approval could expose workspace data; an authenticated but
  unapproved subject must remain in the pending state.

## Implementation Notes

Stage 2 will define this product's client contract, callback/session behavior, subject mapping,
pending-access flow, and failure copy. It will document global Keycloak requirements as an external
dependency without provisioning or operating Keycloak here.
