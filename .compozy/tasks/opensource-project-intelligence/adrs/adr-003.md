# ADR-003: Make Adoption Guidance Deterministic and Policy-Driven

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-20

## Context

The product helps technical leaders decide whether to adopt an open source technology. Raw metrics
alone may not translate an organization's risk tolerance into a decision, while an LLM-generated
verdict would be probabilistic, difficult to reproduce, and inconsistent with the product rule that
numeric metrics and scores remain deterministic. A universal recommendation would also ignore that
the same project can be acceptable for experimentation and unacceptable for critical infrastructure.

## Decision

Adoption guidance will be calculated by deterministic, configurable policies. A policy will evaluate
versioned metric and health inputs against explicit weights, thresholds, required evidence, and
missing-data rules. The resulting recommendation must identify the exact policy version, observation
window, inputs, calculations, and factors that produced it.

LLMs may explain the deterministic result and summarize supporting qualitative evidence. They must
not set metric values, alter policy thresholds, or choose the recommendation.

The product grill will define policy ownership, result categories, default behavior, and the
experience for insufficient data. Stage 2 will define the evaluation model and persistence design.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Evidence Without an Automated Recommendation

- **Description**: Present metrics, trends, risks, and analyses while leaving every adoption verdict
  entirely to the user.
- **Pros**: Avoids encoding organizational preferences and minimizes false certainty.
- **Cons**: Requires every user to repeatedly translate the same evidence into a decision.
- **Why rejected**: The user selected configurable deterministic guidance.

### Alternative 2: AI-Generated Adoption Recommendation

- **Description**: Ask an LLM to classify projects and justify the result with evidence.
- **Pros**: Handles qualitative context flexibly and produces natural explanations.
- **Cons**: Non-reproducible outcomes, prompt/model sensitivity, and risk that narrative confidence
  hides missing or conflicting evidence.
- **Why rejected**: It conflicts with deterministic-first product principles and the selected policy
  model.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Organizations can express different risk tolerances without changing collected facts.
- Recommendations remain reproducible, testable, auditable, and explainable.
- Model outages cannot prevent calculation of an adoption result.

### Negative

- Policy authoring adds configuration and governance complexity.
- Poorly chosen thresholds can produce misleading recommendations despite deterministic execution.
- Metric and policy version changes require explicit result invalidation or recalculation behavior.

### Risks

- Users may treat a configured policy as an objective universal truth; the interface must identify
  policy ownership, scope, version, and limitations next to every result.
- Missing data could be interpreted as failure; the policy model must represent insufficient and
  non-applicable evidence explicitly.

## Implementation Notes

The policy format, lifecycle, permission model, result categories, and recalculation behavior remain
open until the product and surface grills settle them.
